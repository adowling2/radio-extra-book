# Round 7 Technical-Claim Audit — Part 1

## Scope and method

This read-only audit covers Chapters 1–14, the front matter, and the appendices that restate their technical formulas or claims. It follows the Phase 6 protocol in [revision-plan-round7.md](../revision-plan-round7.md): re-derive algebraic and numerical claims where feasible; inspect units, signs, assumptions, and limiting cases; check examination-pool statements against the tracked pool extracts; and distinguish a factual error from a deliberately simplified teaching model.

The audit was originally read-only; its status column now records the scoped Round 7 resolutions. “Correct” below means correct within the stated or recommended scope, not a claim of exhaustive external verification. The current repository contains derived exam-pool extracts, but not the official NCVEC/FCC source documents needed to independently confirm release status or regulatory assertions.

## Findings requiring action

| ID | Location and claim | Method and evidence | Result | Impact | Recommended action |

| T1 | [Ch. 4, “Second-order lowpass,” lines 153–171](../chapters/04_s_plane_poles_and_zeros.tex): at \(\omega=\omega_0\), \(\lvert G\rvert=Q\), formerly described as “the peak height.” | For \(r=\omega/\omega_0\), \(\lvert G\rvert^2=[(1-r^2)^2+4\zeta^2r^2]^{-1}\). Differentiation gives \(r_{\mathrm{peak}}=\sqrt{1-2\zeta^2}\) when \(\zeta<1/\sqrt2\), and \(\lvert G\rvert_{\mathrm{peak}}=1/[2\zeta\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}]\). At \(r=1\), the value is exactly \(Q=1/(2\zeta)\), but it is not generally the maximum. | **Resolved (Round 7).** The text now distinguishes the on-resonance value from the true maximum and states the \(Q>1/\sqrt2\) condition. | Medium; it affects the interpretation of resonance and later RLC examples. | Implemented as stated. |
| T2 | [Ch. 13, worked series-RLC lowpass, lines 308–321 and 384–405](../chapters/13_rlc_series.tex): capacitor-output peak was reported as exactly \(Q\) at \(f_0\). | This is the same second-order lowpass in T1: \(v_C/u=\omega_0^2/(s^2+(R/L)s+\omega_0^2)\). Its response at \(f_0\) is \(Q\), while its maximum is below \(f_0\) and slightly higher. The chapter correctly distinguishes the exact \(f_0/Q\) bandwidth result for current or \(v_R\) from capacitor output elsewhere. | **Resolved (Round 7).** The generic text now says “near,” and the worked-example prose and caption locate the high-\(Q\) maximum just below \(f_0\), retaining the appropriate approximate height. | Medium. | Implemented as stated. |
| T3 | [Ch. 6, factorization, lines 29–52](../chapters/06_higher_order.tex): every real transfer function is represented as a product of stable real-linear and standard quadratic denominator factors. | Real-coefficient polynomials do factor into real linear terms and irreducible conjugate-pair quadratics. However, a right-half-plane real pole has factor \((s-a)\), not \((s+1/\tau)\); a general transfer function also has a numerator and zeros. | **Correct needed.** The mathematical fact is sound but the scope of the displayed form is overstated. | High; this is a foundational structural claim. | Label the product as a *stable, all-pole* response. Then introduce numerator/zero factors separately and state that the exposition is temporarily focusing on all-pole behavior. |
| T4 | [Ch. 5, feedback limits, lines 166–172 and 190–203](../chapters/05_feedback.tex), reinforced by [Ch. 6, three-lag derivation, lines 112–154](../chapters/06_higher_order.tex): instability “needs three or more poles (or a delay)” and positive gain/phase margins imply closed-loop stability. | The three identical stable-lag derivation is correct: two such lags cannot reach \(-180^\circ\) at finite frequency, while three can. The broad statements do not hold for arbitrary plants with right-half-plane poles/zeros, nonstandard feedback signs, or pre-existing instability. Gain/phase margins are a reliable shorthand for the usual stable-loop class; complete Nyquist analysis also requires the open-loop right-half-plane pole count. | **Qualification needed.** | Medium–high; readers may apply an introductory criterion outside its domain. | Put the “stable, strictly proper all-pole cascade with ordinary negative feedback” condition adjacent to the three-pole statement. Qualify the margin statement as applying to the stable open-loop setting developed here; point readers to full Nyquist encirclement accounting otherwise. |
| T5 | [Ch. 6, delay, lines 156–174](../chapters/06_higher_order.tex): any loop with real delay can oscillate, and matched transmission line propagation alone is linked with resonances. | \(e^{-sT}\) has unit magnitude and phase \(-\omega T\), so the equations are correct. A *fixed* delayed loop still needs sufficient loop gain and the remaining phase condition to oscillate. A matched line supplies delay but no standing-wave resonance; reflections or boundary conditions create line resonances. | **Qualification needed.** | Medium. | Say delay supplies unbounded phase lag and *can permit* oscillation when gain and phase conditions are met. Separate propagation delay from resonance due to mismatch/reflection. |
| T6 | [Ch. 6, group delay, lines 245–269](../chapters/06_higher_order.tex) and [glossary, line 25](../appendices/glossary.tex): constant group delay means an undistorted waveform. | \(\tau_g=-d\phi/d\omega\), and constant group delay corresponds to linear phase, correctly. An undistorted time-domain waveform also needs suitably flat magnitude response over the waveform’s occupied band (and an affine phase there). | **Qualification needed.** | Medium. | Add the flat-magnitude / occupied-band condition in Chapter 6 and repeat it in the glossary definition. |
| T7 | [Ch. 7, skin effect](../chapters/07_circuit_modeling.tex): RF conductors are “stranded, tubular, or silver-plated” to reduce skin-effect loss. | The skin-depth derivation and copper numerical values follow from \(\delta=\sqrt{2\rho/(\omega\mu)}\) under the stated \(a\gg\delta\) approximation. Ordinary stranded wire does not necessarily reduce skin-effect resistance; individually insulated, suitably sized **Litz wire** is the relevant construction. Tubular conductors and surface plating have appropriate high-frequency uses. | **Resolved (Round 7).** | Medium; it could teach an incorrect mitigation. | Replaced “stranded” with Litz wire and retained the geometry/frequency qualification. |
| T8 | [Ch. 7, inductor self-resonance](../chapters/07_circuit_modeling.tex) and [formula index](../appendices/formula_index.tex): \(Q_{\mathrm{eff}}\to0\) at \(f_{\mathrm{SRF}}\); \(f_{\mathrm{peak}}=f_{\mathrm{SRF}}/\sqrt5\). | The exact stated expression is \(Q_{\mathrm{eff}}=Q_0[(1-(f/f_{\mathrm{SRF}})^2)-R^2C_p/L]\). Therefore its zero is shifted by the retained loss term, not exactly the lossless \(f_{\mathrm{SRF}}\). The \(f_{\mathrm{peak}}\) result follows after the chapter’s high-\(Q\), frequency-scaling approximations. | **Resolved (Round 7).** | Medium. | Identified the lossless/high-\(Q\) approximations in the prose and formula index. |
| T9 | [Ch. 11, RC complementary transfers](../chapters/11_rc_circuits.tex): \(G_C+G_R=1\), paraphrased as “whatever the capacitor does not pass, the resistor does.” | The complex identity is exact. It is not an amplitude complement: at the corner frequency both magnitudes are \(1/\sqrt2\). The power-like identity \(\lvert G_C\rvert^2+\lvert G_R\rvert^2=1\) is the safer magnitude statement. | **Resolved (Round 7).** | Low. | Named the relation as a complex-transfer identity and rejected the magnitude-sum interpretation. |
| T10 | [Ch. 12, common-mode choke](../chapters/12_rl_circuits.tex): differential current passes “untouched” and common-mode current sees the full inductance. | The flux-cancellation and flux-addition argument is correct for the ideal, symmetrical winding model. Real devices have leakage inductance, winding resistance, capacitance, imperfect balance, saturation limits, and a finite useful frequency range. | **Resolved (Round 7).** | Low–medium. | Qualified the ideal statements and named leakage inductance and winding capacitance. |
| T11 | [Ch. 14, cross-chapter comparison](../chapters/14_rlc_parallel.tex): the preceding series and parallel examples have “the same pole pair.” | Their \(L\) and \(C\), hence \(\omega_0\), match, but the examples use different resistances and therefore different \(Q\) values (about 43 versus 84). Their damping and pole pairs differ. | **Resolved (Round 7).** | Low. | Replaced the shared-pole claim with shared natural frequency/second-order form and different damping/poles. |

## Examination-pool checks

The tracked pool extracts support the following local checks:

- [Extra E5D](../references/extra-pool-circuits.md) contains the twelve questions cited around Chapter 7’s skin-effect discussion. They ask for qualitative direction/cause, conductor practice, parasitics, phase, reactance, and power concepts; they do not ask candidates to derive the skin-depth exponent. The chapter’s limited assertion is supported by the tracked extract.
- Searches of the tracked General and Extra circuit extracts found no questions containing the technical vocabulary used for damping ratio, root locus, Bode plot, gain margin, or phase margin. “Not examined” statements should remain bounded to the named/current pool and should not be generalized to all licensing or engineering examinations.

The repository’s [pool README](../references/README.md) describes the files as curated, derived study aids. They are appropriate internal evidence for what this book’s tracked extracts contain, but not a substitute for an official pool release or errata record.

## Recommended disposition

T1–T11 were resolved in Round 7 with local corrections or scope qualifications. Hold T12 for an official-source check rather than adding an unverified citation. No claim in this audit calls for a change to the textbook’s central first-order, RLC, phasor, or circuit-analysis derivations.
